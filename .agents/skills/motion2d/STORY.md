# STORY: how to write a reel story that engages

## What the director wants
- **Story before characters.** A unique story comes first.
- **Real and grounded.** Strange things happen in a normal place that feels real (a Japanese town, rules, objects
  that are not allowed, the lone survivor). High-concept gimmicks were rejected ("None of these interests me").
- **One concept people engage with**, not a mix of ideas. It needs suspense and rules, and something that grows each episode.
- **Length:** an episode is 45-65 s (the director said 15-21 s "is not something that will engage").
  Reels in a series can mix 8-10 s "log" dread clips with 45-65 s story episodes.
- Narrator plus a main character. In 5 O'CLOCK, Sam is the hero.
- Tone references (Chainsaw Man, Pacific Rim, Kaiju No. 8) mean energy and scale, not copying.

## The spine (keep it)
One hero, one place, one clock, one rule per episode, one thing that gets bigger every episode.
Example: "The 5 PM chime was never meant for children. Kiriyama built it in 1964 to keep a door shut. This year, the other side learned the song."

## Beat-sheet skeleton (45-65 s, 12-18 shots, 9:16)
| Beat | Time | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0-3 s | One wrong detail in a normal image, readable without sound (mirrored clock digits, a chime that stops at 3 notes) |
| Normal world | 3-15 s | The place and the hero's routine, with the small wrongnesses planted |
| Turn | 15-25 s | The rule breaks; the hero is the only one who notices |
| Escalation | 25-45 s | Each shot raises the stakes: the town reacts, the rule is tested, something big is shown in pieces |
| Spectacle | 45-55 s | The "crazy" moment: the creature or world shown huge, with multi-plane scale and synced motion |
| Cliffhanger | 55-62 s | A personal stake (mother, double, choice) |
| Stinger | last 2-3 s | A short dark beat, then one final image or line that lands (a new rule appearing in the hero's handwriting) |

Every shot needs real animation, a camera idea, and one "wow" detail. Put one hidden rewatch detail in the episode
(a reflection half a second late, a sweeper off the beat looking at the camera).

## Series devices that tested well (research)
- Numbered logs ("KIRIYAMA · DAY 004 · 17:00"): the strongest serial format found (a 4.9M-view example).
- A rules notebook, two contradicting rule lists, viewer-suggested rules, a pinned Day 1.
- A caption footer: "Hand-drawn. No AI." plus a question ("Would you answer?").

## Dialogue script format (written in Phase 1, recorded in Phase 6)
Keep it sparse: about 8-11 lines per 60 s, so the pictures carry it. Lines are 1.5-3 s, with 1-3 s gaps.
| Time (s) | Shot | Who | Line | Delivery |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.4 | 02 | Narrator | "On the other side, everything is the same." | deep, warm, close |
Narrator: a deep, warm, older (not too old) man, close-mic, unhurried, like telling a secret.

## Show it as pictures
Phase 1 output is a storyboard page (one frame per shot, a timecode, one line of action, one line of dialogue)
plus the shot list file. Keep the text short.

## Never
- Re-pitch rejected ideas.
- Copy the art-form reference's characters, scenes or plot.
- Write walls of text for the director.
