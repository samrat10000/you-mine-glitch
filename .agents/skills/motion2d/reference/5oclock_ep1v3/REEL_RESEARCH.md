# 5 O’CLOCK reel research

22 November 2026. Read-only Instagram review in logged-in Chrome, with public web sources for Japanese setting and cross-platform examples. Uses the story premise in the handoff. The separate story files were still pending when checked.

## What to develop

Build around **the town’s 5 PM chime receiving an answer that changes what Sam does next**. The road reel already makes some viewers imagine a connection between worlds. The opportunity for this series is to make contact observable and consequential. A viewer should be able to point to an exact sound, timing discrepancy, or physical reaction as evidence and still disagree about who is answering. Start with something Sam can test. Use later episodes to change the meaning of a clue the audience has already seen. Kaiju can become part of the payoff once the audience understands what the signal does.

Each numbered episode should work when encountered out of order. Give it a concrete incident, one result, and a reason to watch the next one. An episode number helps people navigate an existing story. Its contribution to reach is unproven. Put the title and number on the cover and repeat them briefly in the reel or its ending, with the anomaly itself getting the opening frame.

## Japanese reference and cross-platform comparison

- **The real routine:** Kawasaki City describes a daily melody test around 5 PM, for approximately a minute, to check its outdoor disaster broadcast system. Wakayama lists a 5 PM scheduled broadcast and explains its rooftop transmitter feeds outdoor speakers around the city. Its guidance notes that distance, buildings and wind affect audibility. For the fictional town, establish where Sam can hear the normal chime. A real acoustical baseline makes an impossible reply easier to recognize. An original chime can provide a recurring rhythm and give the sound designer control over its echo. ## Prototype brief for when the story arrives

Make a 12–15 second storyboard or animatic from one approved story beat. Use an original Japanese street layout, a visibly established loudspeaker, and a hand-drawn Sam reaction. In the first second, show the source of the daily routine and plant a readable inconsistency. In the middle, let Sam test what he just heard. At the end, show a clear result that alters his next choice. For example, if the script allows it, a second signal can answer a deliberate interruption. The example is a test of the premise and leaves the storyteller to choose the actual rule.

1. **Opening test.** Cut two versions from the same shots. In A, give the normal chime a breath before the answer. In B, open on the answer or its visible consequence and reveal the normal cue immediately after. Match duration, ending, art and cover. On a phone, ask cold viewers what happened and which second made them want to keep watching. Run a muted viewing and check that the anomalous event still reads.
2. **Ending test.** Compare a hard cut after a complete incident with a cut that returns to the opening composition and exposes a new implication. Check whether viewers understand the result and can name the unanswered question. If they only ask what happened, clarify the action. If they can state what happened and offer different explanations, preserve that ambiguity.
3. **Sound and craft.** Build a clean close-miked human performance if the beat needs a line. Give the town ambience, broadcast and reply distinct sources. Test through a small phone speaker and headphones. Keep the chime recognizable in captions or visible action. A rough-to-final clip and specific performer and artist credits can document craft alongside the finished series.
4. **Episode path.** Use a consistent *5 O’CLOCK* title and visible numbering. Pin a real episode as the entry point when the series is live. Give each caption one plain route to Episode 1 or the next release. Where the platform shows and supports a pinned comment, use it for the same path. The repeated Granite Waves and Kowazo questions show the cost of leaving navigation only in a collapsed caption.
5. **Measurement.** On owned posts, record runtime, date, organic or paid status, accounts reached, average watch time, the retention curve or early hold if available, shares and follows attributed to each reel. Use shares and follows per 1,000 accounts reached for comparisons. Keep the same 24-hour and 7-day windows. Preserve raw watch time and report average watch time divided by runtime as a descriptive ratio. Read completion from the actual end-of-video metric or curve when available. Repeat a promising edit choice across several comparable episodes before calling it a format effect. Code comment themes by theory, scene or dialogue, craft, continuation intent, navigation, and confusion. Track requests for the title or viewing path separately from theories about the story.
