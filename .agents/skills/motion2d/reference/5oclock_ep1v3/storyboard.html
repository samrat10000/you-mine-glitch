五時のチャイム5 O'CLOCK
Episode 1: The Fourth Note

Every town in Japan plays a song at five o'clock. Kiriyama's has four notes. Today it stops after three, and Sam, the only one wearing headphones, is the only one who notices which side of the mirror he's on.

63 s · 9:16 vertical 18 shots Voices: narrator, Sam, mirror-Sam, Grandma Hoshino Music + voice added after the animation is locked

Full dialogue script

Sparse on purpose: 11 lines in 63 seconds, so the pictures carry it. Recorded and mixed only after every shot is approved. The music thins under each line and swells back in the gaps.

Cast and world

Sam, 17

Lean, narrow shoulders, slightly too-long hair, big over-ear headphones, oversized grey hoodie with the strings uneven, konbini bag on the handlebar. Quiet, a little sarcastic. Loses something every episode.

Mirror-Sam

Identical, except his hair parts the other way, the hoodie strings are swapped, and his pupils never quite catch the light. He moves a half beat early. He has been waiting for Sam a long time.

Grandma Hoshino

Runs the tobacco and sweets shop on the corner. Tiny, bent, sharp eyes, bamboo broom. She knows the rules and has followed them for 62 years. She's the one who tried to warn him.

The Listener

A colossal shape on the other side of the sky, taller than the mountain. Its head is a crown of hundreds of rusted Showa-era loudspeaker horns that all turn together, like a field of sunflowers following a sound. Long arms with too many joints. It learned the song.

The townsfolk

Kids with randoseru backpacks, a salaryman on his phone, a girl on a bike, the shopkeeper pulling his shutter. Each one has a small 5 PM ritual: ears covered, mirror turned to the wall, face away from the street.

Kiriyama

A mountain valley town on the sea, one train line, rice fields. Orange curve mirrors on every corner, Showa loudspeakers on poles, the 1964 radio tower with a blinking red light, a lone vending machine, a railway crossing.

Hidden detail for rewatches

In shot 04, Sam passes a shop window. His reflection is half a second late. Nobody will catch it the first time. The comments will.

How it gets made

  1. One world, one look. The town, the mirror world and every character are built and animated together, so nothing looks pasted on. Ink lines, flat cel colour with one shadow tone, painted skies, soft depth of field, and the one red accent.
  2. Real animation, not slides. Animation on twos, held on threes for weight, smears on fast moves. Hair, hoodie strings, bag, leaves and crows all have follow-through. Every human move is checked against real reference footage first, so hands and limbs stay right.
  3. Hardest shots first. Shots 08 (the frozen-town fly-through) and 15 (the Listener) get built first as 2-second tests. You see them as full-quality clips and full-size PNGs before anything else is made.
  4. Shot by shot. Each shot comes to you as its own clip plus PNGs. Nothing gets cut together until you've passed it.
  5. Sound last. Once all 18 shots are approved, the voices are recorded, then the piano score is written, mixed around the lines.
